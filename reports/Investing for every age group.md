# Pakistan opens investing doors at every age

As of October 2026, Pakistanis at every age have a regulated way to save or invest, and the ladder got noticeably longer in the past year. **Since 1 April 2026, teenagers aged 13 to 18 can own and operate their own bank account or digital wallet** under SBP BPRD Circular No. 01 of 2026. **Since November 2025, a parent or guardian can open a PSX "Minor Investor Account" for a child.** From 18, a CNIC unlocks digital bank accounts, low-limit "Sahulat" and "Sehl" mutual fund and stock accounts, and the Voluntary Pension Scheme (VPS). For retirees, the government's National Savings (CDNS) welfare products pay **12.96% a year from 21 October 2026**. Buying foreign stocks is legal for residents, but only through one narrow door: **up to USD 25,000 per calendar year, wired through a single bank branch you designate, by an active taxpayer, with dividends and sale proceeds brought back to Pakistan**. Most of the popular workarounds (topping up a foreign broker with a card, Wise, Payoneer or a freelancer balance) are grey. Offshore forex and CFD apps, unlicensed "brokers" pitched on WhatsApp, and unlicensed crypto providers are prohibited or illegal. A free education site has to explain all of this to a country where **about 4 in 10 people aged 10 and over cannot read and only about a quarter of adults pass basic money questions**. That means short Urdu-first text, examples in rupees rather than percentages, and a "pakka / pakka nahin" (guaranteed / not guaranteed) label on every product.

A caveat runs through everything below. Several primary documents (the full text of the SBP teen-account circular, SECP press release PDFs, and pages on savings.gov.pk) blocked automated access during research. Some details therefore rest on press coverage of the official document, and the gaps are flagged where they matter.

## Teenagers got their own bank accounts in 2026, but they can only invest through a guardian

The biggest change for 14-to-17-year-olds is that they no longer need a parent to *operate* their bank account. SBP's April 2026 framework applies to every bank, microfinance bank and electronic money institution, so it covers wallet providers such as Easypaisa and JazzCash as well as banks ([SBP press release](https://www.sbp.org.pk/press/2026/Pr-01-Apr-2026-1.pdf); [PKRevenue](https://pkrevenue.com/pakistan-allows-teen-bank-accounts-sbp-new-framework/)). A teen can open the account alone or jointly. It holds **PKR only** and is a savings account by default. It comes with a debit card and app banking, but **no cheque book, credit or overdraft**, and it can receive remittances from abroad, paid out in rupees ([Arab News Pakistan](https://www.arabnews.pk/pakistan/pakistan-central-bank-allows-teenagers-to-independently-own-operate-bank-accounts-2638487)). The bank must open it as soon as the requirements are met and never take more than 2 working days. At 18 the same account converts into a regular one once the holder completes full KYC, which in practice means getting a CNIC ([SBP circular extract](https://www.sbp.org.pk/bprd/2026/C1.htm)). SBP puts the eligible population at **about 26 million people** ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/04/01/sbp-announces-independent-bank-accounts-for-teenagers/amp/)). Three details remain unconfirmed because the circular text could not be read: the exact ID a teen must show, whether a guardian must consent, and any balance or transaction limits.

The older route, a guardian-operated "minor" account, still exists. Examples include Bank Alfalah's SnaPack Kids, Allied Bank's Rising Star, Soneri Youngsters (which also has an Islamic version), Bank AL Habib Young Savers and Dubai Islamic Bank's junior account. These typically need the child's NADRA B-Form, Juvenile Card or birth certificate, plus the guardian's CNIC ([Bank Alfalah T&C](https://www.bankalfalah.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/SnaPack-Kids-AC-TC-19-09-24-1.pdf); [ABL](https://www.abl.com/personal/accounts/exclusive-account/allied-rising-star-account/); [DIB Pakistan](https://www.dibpak.com/index.php/wp-json/wp/v2/pages/273)). Alfalah's terms show one detail families should know: the account must be closed or converted at 18 if a natural guardian opened it, but at 21 if the guardian was appointed by a court.

Investing is a separate matter, and here the guardian stays in control. Under guidelines that PSX, NCCPL and CDC issued with SECP in November 2025, a stock account for a minor is titled "Name of Minor (Minor) through Name of Guardian (Guardian)". **The guardian has exclusive authority** and goes through the source-of-funds checks. The minor's Juvenile Card, Form-B or Child Registration Certificate is required, plus a court guardianship certificate if the guardian is not the father. Futures, margin and leveraged products, and same-day trading are all barred. At 18 the account freezes for new trades until the young person opens one in their own name. The shares then move across at their original cost and date, with **no capital gains tax on the transfer** ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/11/06/psx-issues-new-guidelines-for-opening-trading-accounts-of-minors/amp/); [SECP press release](https://www.secp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Press-Release-Guidelines-Issued-for-Trading-Accounts-by-Minors-in-the-Capital-Markets.pdf)). Minors **cannot open a VPS pension account**, because entry starts at 18 ([EFU Life](https://www.efulife.com/assets/documents/EFU-Hemayah-Pension-Fund.pdf)). The research could not find current rules on whether mutual fund units or ordinary National Savings certificates can be held in a minor's name. The only confirmed CDNS route is Behbood certificates for a "special minor" (a child with a disability) through a guardian ([savings.gov.pk](https://savings.gov.pk/behbood-savings-certificate/)). The honest message for teens is simple: you can now bank on your own, your parent or guardian can invest for you, and it all moves into your name at 18.

## A CNIC at 18 unlocks the whole regulated toolkit, and VPS is the tax lever for workers

Turning 18 and getting a CNIC opens almost every domestic product. Freelancers have a dedicated **Freelancer Digital Account** in rupees or foreign currency. It allows up to USD 5,000 a month each way, needs only a CNIC and due-diligence checks, and the bank must decide within 2 working days ([SBP annex](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/document/circulars/C2-Annex-A_5.pdf)). Some banks link it to an Exporters' Special Foreign Currency Account (ESFCA), whose funds can be converted to rupees but not moved to another foreign-currency account ([Bank AL Habib FAQ](https://bankalhabib.com/files/documents/freelancer-account-faq.pdf); [SBP 2026 annexure](https://www.sbp.org.pk/epd/2026/FECL6-Annexure.pdf)). For first-time investors, the **PSX Sahulat Account** opens through a broker with only a CNIC copy and allows purchases of up to **PKR 3 million** ([PSX](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/resources-and-tools/investors/sahulat-account)). In May 2026 SECP raised the limits on low-risk mutual fund accounts: the **Sehl account went from PKR 200,000 to PKR 1 million**, and the **Sahulat account from PKR 1 million to PKR 3 million**. It also simplified digital onboarding ([Nukta](https://nukta.com/secp-raises-limits-mutual-funds); [Dunya News](https://dunyanews.tv/en/Business/951659-secp-eases-digital-mutual-fund-investing-rules-for-small-investors)). For Shariah-conscious savers, Meezan runs a PSX-listed ETF that tracks a Pakistani Shariah index ([Sarmaaya](https://sarmaaya.pk/mutual-funds/8043a7d9-4671-4fa5-b520-b9da520f1b45)), and Mahaana operates as an SECP-licensed, digital-only asset manager ([Mahaana](https://www.mahaana.com/archive/about)).

For salaried adults aged roughly 26 to 45, the VPS is the product that pays for itself in tax. Under Section 63, contributions earn a tax credit on up to **20% of taxable income** or the amount actually invested, whichever is lower ([HBL AMC](https://hblasset.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/VPS-Tax-2025.pdf); [MCB Funds](https://www.mcbfunds.com/tax-bachao/)). Shariah-compliant versions exist, such as the EFU Hemayah Pension Fund ([EFU Life](https://www.efulife.com/hemayah-takaful/retirement-solutions/hemayah-pension-fund)). Sources disagree on exactly who qualifies for the credit (any CNIC holder, or only people with Pakistani taxable income), so the site should link to the current Income Tax Ordinance rather than state a rule. The research did not confirm minimum investments for specific apps, or the tax treatment for students and non-filers. Those are open items for the site's editors.

## For 60+, government welfare savings pay 12.96% and VPS releases half its balance tax-free

Retirees and people close to retirement have a separate set of products. CDNS runs three welfare schemes: **Behbood Savings Certificates** (for senior citizens, widows and people with disabilities), the **Pensioners' Benefit Account** (for retired pensioners, minimum Rs 10,000, maximum Rs 5 million according to the official page), and the **Shuhada Family Welfare Account** (no zakat deducted). All three rose **from 12.72% to 12.96% a year on 21 October 2026** ([savings.gov.pk PBA](https://savings.gov.pk/pensioners-benefit-account/); [The Pakistan Connect](https://thepakistanconnect.com/national-savings-new-profit-rates/); [savings.gov.pk Shuhada](https://savings.gov.pk/shuhadas-family-welfare-account/)). These rates change often, so the site should link to savings.gov.pk rather than hard-code a number. The research could not confirm the age at which Behbood treats someone as a "senior citizen" (commonly said to be 60), or Behbood's minimum and maximum amounts. A 2024 article cited a Rs 7.5 million maximum for the Pensioners' Benefit Account, which conflicts with the official Rs 5 million figure; the site should use the official one.

People who have built up a VPS balance can retire from it between ages 60 and 70, and some funds also allow retirement after 25 years in the scheme. **Up to 50% of the balance can be withdrawn tax-free**, the rest is taxed, and early withdrawals are taxable ([NBP Funds](https://nbpfunds.com/?p=36606); [SBP Pension FAQs](https://www.sbp.org.pk/RDAC/Pension-FAQs.html)). The natural way to present this is VPS as the tool for building a lump sum before retirement, and Behbood or the Pensioners' Benefit Account as the "steady monthly income" tool after it.

| Age | What they can open | Who controls it | Key limit or feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13–17 | Own teen bank account or wallet (since Apr 2026) | The teen | PKR only; debit card; no cheque book or overdraft |
| Under 18 | Guardian-operated minor bank account | Guardian | Converts at 18 (21 if court-appointed guardian, per Alfalah) |
| Under 18 | PSX Minor Investor Account (since Nov 2025) | Guardian only | No leverage or futures; moves to own name at 18 with no CGT on transfer |
| 18+ | Freelancer Digital Account, PSX Sahulat, mutual fund Sehl / Sahulat, VPS | Self | USD 5k a month (FDA); PKR 3m (PSX Sahulat); PKR 1m / 3m (Sehl / Sahulat) |
| Workers | VPS | Self | Tax credit on up to 20% of taxable income |
| 60+ and welfare groups | Behbood, Pensioners' Benefit, Shuhada accounts | Self | 12.96% a year from 21 Oct 2026 |
| 60–70 | VPS retirement | Self | Up to 50% of balance withdrawable tax-free |

## Foreign stocks have one legal door, a wide grey hallway, and locked rooms

Can a Pakistani teenager or retiree legally buy Apple or Tesla shares? Yes, but the legal route is narrow. Chapter 20 of SBP's Foreign Exchange Manual, in text footnoted to FE Circular No. 01 of 11 July 2024, gives "designated authorized dealers" (banks) a **general permission to send money abroad for resident individuals to buy shares of listed companies, up to USD 25,000 per calendar year**, with no more than 1% of any single company. The conditions are strict. The money must be legitimate and tax-paid. The investor must be **on the active taxpayer list** and have a clean loan record. Everything must go through **one bank branch the investor designates**, with that designation acknowledged by SBP. Investing in India needs prior SBP approval. Within a month the investor must submit evidence of the purchase and file **form V-100**, and all **dividends and sale proceeds must be brought back to Pakistan** and converted to rupees ([SBP FE Manual Ch. 20](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/document/Chapter-20-foreign-exchange-manual.pdf)). This window dates from SBP's 2021 liberalisation, which ended case-by-case approval ([The News](https://www.thenews.com.pk/print/788129-sbp-exempts-resident-investors-from-approval-for-foreign-investment)). One widely shared guide claims a USD 50,000 limit, but that figure applies only to employee stock option plans for staff of foreign companies' Pakistani subsidiaries ([AbroadMate](https://abroadmate.me/guides/how-to-invest-from-pakistan-2026) vs [SBP FE Manual](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/document/Chapter-20-foreign-exchange-manual.pdf)). In practice the US State Department describes Pakistan's outward-investment approvals as "cumbersome and time-consuming" ([US State Dept](https://www.state.gov/reports/2025-investment-climate-statements/pakistan)). The research did not establish how many banks actually offer the designated-branch service to ordinary customers.

Two practical points follow from the wording. First, the rule permits buying "shares of listed companies". Whether a US-listed index ETF counts is not stated, because ETF units are fund units, not company shares. The honest answer is "ask your designated bank". Second, the repatriation duty sits uneasily with how most buy-and-hold investors behave. Reinvesting dividends abroad, or leaving sale proceeds in a foreign broker account, appears to breach the condition. On tax, residents are taxed on worldwide income. Anyone with **foreign income of USD 10,000 or more, or foreign assets worth USD 100,000 or more, must file a foreign income and assets statement** under Section 116A ([FBR](https://www.fbr.gov.pk/section-116A/152720)). The exact capital gains rate on foreign shares for tax year 2026/27 was not confirmed from primary law.

No foreign retail broker holds an SECP licence. Some, notably Interactive Brokers, accept Pakistani clients, and the legal way to fund such an account is the designated-bank wire described above ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/how-to-trade-us-stocks-from-pakistan-without-a-us-bank-account-5-best-platforms-in-2026/); [Elphinstone](https://elphinstone.us/is-it-legal-for-pakistanis-to-invest-in-the-us/)). Community posts show that many people fund such accounts through Wise or other indirect routes ([Reddit](https://reddit.com/r/pakistan/comments/165kt45/investing_in_the_us_stock_market/)). That is where the grey zone begins. SBP caps card-based cross-border spending at **USD 30,000 a year per person and bars card payments for commercial purposes** ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40245252/card-based-cross-border-transactions-authorised-dealers-allowed-to-purchase-usd-from-inter-bank-market)). A broker deposit is an investment, not a purchase of goods or services, so card, Wise or Payoneer funding at best skirts the rules. The research found no official SBP or FBR statement on funding from freelancer foreign-currency balances. The manual explicitly allows ESFCA funds to be used for *companies'* equity investment abroad, not for individuals.

The prohibited territory is clearer. **SBP's May 2022 circular letter bans sending foreign exchange through any channel to offshore online forex, margin or CFD trading platforms**, and tells banks to block such payments ([SBP EPD CL 8 of 2022](https://www.sbp.org.pk/epd/2022/FECL8.htm)). That ban applies even when the platform is regulated in its home country. Crypto now has its own law: the **Virtual Assets Act 2026** made PVARA the permanent licensing authority. Reported penalties for unlicensed providers reach **PKR 50 million and five years in prison**, and banks may serve only PVARA-licensed firms ([CCN](https://www.ccn.com/news/crypto/pakistan-crypto-ban-regulation-passes-virtual-assets-act-2026/); [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40379729)). Offshore crypto exchanges launched USDT-settled "tokenised stock" futures on Apple and Tesla in October 2026 ([TradingView/PrimeXBT](https://www.tradingview.com/news/tradingview:4366169865a85:0-primexbt-launches-tokenised-stocks-on-pxtrader-2-0/)). These are derivatives, not ownership, and most likely fall foul of both the CFD ban and PVARA licensing.

Inside Pakistan, the regulated substitutes are thin. Local mutual funds may invest up to 30% abroad, capped at **US$15 million per fund**, with case-by-case SBP approval ([SBP FE Manual Ch. 20](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/document/Chapter-20-foreign-exchange-manual.pdf)). The research did not confirm any widely sold Pakistani global-equity or S&P 500 fund for residents. PMEX, which SECP regulates, has been approved since 2017 to list cash-settled futures on the Dow, NASDAQ-100 and S&P 500 ([SECP](https://www.secp.gov.pk/media-center/press-releases/secp-approves-us-equity-index-futures-contracts-for-trading-through-pmex/)). Those are short-dated leveraged contracts, a tool for speculating or hedging rather than a buy-and-hold index fund. Overseas Pakistanis are a separate case: the USD 25,000 rule covers "residents of Pakistan", so a Pakistani living abroad who invests money earned abroad follows the rules of the country where they live ([SBP FE Manual Ch. 20](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/document/Chapter-20-foreign-exchange-manual.pdf)).

| Status | Route |
|---|---|
| **Legal** | Wiring up to USD 25k a year in listed foreign shares through one designated bank branch, as an active taxpayer, with V-100 reporting and proceeds brought home; employee stock plans (USD 50k); PMEX US index futures through a PMEX-licensed broker; local funds with SBP-approved foreign allocations |
| **Grey** | Funding a foreign broker by card, Wise, Payoneer, a freelancer balance or friends abroad; buying ETFs under the "listed companies" permission; keeping dividends or proceeds abroad |
| **Illegal / prohibited** | Remitting to offshore forex, CFD or margin platforms; any unlicensed "broker" offering PSX or international shares; unlicensed crypto service providers; offshore tokenised-stock derivatives |
| **Scam** | WhatsApp or Telegram "account managers", guaranteed or daily returns, fake "IBKR" or broker look-alikes, upfront "withdrawal tax" demands |

## Scammers borrow real brand names, so verification has to be the first lesson

Pakistan's most common investment frauds copy the look of legitimate firms. In November 2024 and again in May 2025, **SECP warned that a platform calling itself "Interactive Brokers Group (IBKR)" was not licensed**. It was promoted through social media and WhatsApp with promises of high returns ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2024/11/15/secp-warns-public-against-another-illegal-trading-platform/); [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/05/22/secp-warns-public-against-investing-in-unlicensed-online-trading-platforms)). The warning targets a scam using the IBKR name, not the real broker, but it shows how easily a foreign brand becomes bait. In April 2025 SECP declared Quotex unauthorised and referred it to the FIA, PTA and Google for blocking ([APP](https://www.app.com.pk/national/secp-cautions-public-against-illegal-offshore-trading-investment-platforms/)). In December 2025 it described the standard pattern: fake platforms that impersonate licensed brokers, show **made-up profit dashboards, allow small early withdrawals to build trust, then push victims to deposit more** ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/12/09/secp-warns-public-of-rising-fraud-as-fake-trading-platforms-impersonate-licensed-brokers/)). SECP's advice is to check every broker against the licensed lists on the SECP, PSX and PMEX websites. The research found no 2026 SECP warning list, so the site should link to SECP's live press releases rather than copy a list that will go out of date. Red flags worth teaching are any offer of "US stocks" over WhatsApp, deposits to a personal bank account, Easypaisa, JazzCash or a USDT wallet, guaranteed returns, and fees demanded before you can withdraw.

## The website must write for 5 to 8 years of schooling, in rupees, in Urdu first

Pakistan's audience sets a high bar for clarity. Census 2023 puts literacy among people aged 10 and over at **60.65%: 68% for men, 52.84% for women, and 51.56% in rural areas**, barely up from 58.91% in 2017 ([Wikipedia / PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_Pakistan)). Only **26% of adults** answered at least three of four basic financial-concept questions correctly, against 33% worldwide ([SBP NFER 2025-29](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/document/NFER-DOC_1.pdf)). Global Findex 2025 data show just **11.9% of women own an account, against 42.3% of men** ([FinStatGlobe](https://data.finstatglobe.com/blog/state-of-financial-inclusion-2026)). Only 24% of women own a phone, against 89% of men ([Gallup Pakistan](https://galluppakistandigitalanalytics.com/2026/01/28/pakistans-gender-gap-in-mobile-phone-ownership-a-major-barrier-to-financial-inclusion/)). SBP's own banking statistics give a much better inclusion picture than Findex, because SBP counts CNIC-linked accounts while Findex surveys individuals, so the site should name the measure whenever it quotes a figure. Notably, **SBP places stock exchanges, asset managers and inflation in its "Advanced" literacy tier** ([SBP NFER](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/document/NFER-DOC_1.pdf)). An investment site therefore starts at the level SBP considers hardest.

The right target comes from established standards. WCAG 2.2 SC 3.1.5 asks for a simpler version of any text that needs more than **lower secondary education (7 to 9 years of school)**, and supports a plain summary at the top of each page ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/reading-level)). GOV.UK-based guides aim for a reading age of 9 ([Surrey County Council](https://www.surreycc.gov.uk/website/writing-guide/reading-levels-for-web-pages)). W3C's cognitive accessibility guidance tells writers to "avoid relying on numerical or mathematical skills" ([W3C COGA](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o3-clear-content/)). The UK's FCA requires firms to test their communications, including with small groups of 3 to 5 people who share a particular need ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/consumer-understanding-good-practice-areas-improvement)). English readability formulas do not work for Urdu, so the practical method is to have a Pakistani teacher check each page, then test it with a 14-year-old, a home-based woman and a retiree.

Pakistan-specific vocabulary is the subtlest trap. SBP's FAQ records scholarly consensus that interest is riba ([SBP](https://www.sbp.org.pk/faqs/faqs-islamic-banking)). As a result, both Islamic and conventional products, including National Savings, advertise "profit rates" or "expected returns" ([Daily Times](https://dailytimes.com.pk/1570322/pakistan-revises-profit-rates-for-national-savings-schemes/)). To many readers, "munafa" sounds both halal and guaranteed. So every product card should say whether the amount is **fixed in advance or can go up and down**, with a "pakka / pakka nahin" badge, and should mark expected returns as "not promised". Lakh and crore should be shown next to millions. "Maturity" should become "end date, when you get your money back". A mutual fund can be compared with a committee (BC), but the site must add that it is regulated and not guaranteed. The Urdu equivalents in the research notes are common usage that has not been checked: SECP's 1,050-term English–Urdu glossary ([SECP](https://www.secp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/SECP-publishes-glossary.pdf)) could not be opened, so a native editor should check the wording against it.

|---|---|---|
| Expected return | What they hope to pay. Not promised | mutawaqqa munafa |
| Real return | What you really gain after prices go up | mehngai ke baad asal munafa |
| Diversification | Don't put all your money in one place | saare ande aik tokri mein na rakhein |
| Liquidity | How quickly you can get your cash back | paisa kitni jaldi nikal sakta hai |
| Guaranteed / not guaranteed | Promised in full / could be less | pakka / pakka nahin |

One design can serve both ends of the age range. Nielsen Norman Group's research with 123 users aged 65 and over found that small, light text, tiny tap targets, rigid input formats and unclear error messages cause the most trouble ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-for-senior-citizens/)). Its research with 100 teens found that lower reading levels and impatience demand simpler sites, and that teens "dislike tiny font sizes as much as adults do" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-of-websites-for-teenagers/)). Large dark text, short chunks, big buttons and inputs that accept "10,000", "10000" or "10 lakh" therefore help everyone. On top of that shared base, teens get goal-based examples (a phone, a bike, university fees), quick quizzes and progress bars, building on SBP's POMPAK game, which reached 600,000 students. Readers over 60 get fewer steps, no timeouts, "we never ask for your PIN or OTP" notices and Urdu audio or video, which matches SBP's own scam-prevention focus for that group ([SBP NFER](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/document/NFER-DOC_1.pdf)). The research found no controlled study showing that rupee amounts beat percentages for understanding risk. Still, the regulator guidance and the pension-projection literature all point the same way: write "Rs 10,000 could become between Rs A and Rs B in 5 years", with a bad-year example, and put the percentage in smaller text.

## Conclusion

The regulated ladder now has a rung for every age. The bigger risk is that people never find the right rung, or that a fake one catches them first. A 16-year-old with a new debit card and a 62-year-old with a pension lump sum are both prime targets for the "US stocks on WhatsApp" pitch. That pitch works because the legal route to foreign shares is so narrow and bureaucratic that the grey and illegal routes feel normal. So the most valuable thing a free site can do is not list products. It is to give every reader one repeatable test: is it on the SECP, PSX, PMEX or PVARA list, is it pakka or pakka nahin, and does the money go through your own bank? On foreign investing, the site should be candid. The USD 25,000 designated-bank route is legal but hard. Card and Wise funding is grey, not safe. Leveraged foreign-index products and tokenised stocks are speculation, not ownership.

Three facts were unreachable and should be verified before launch: the exact ID and limits in SBP's teen-account circular, the rules for mutual fund and National Savings holdings in a minor's name, and how the "listed companies" permission applies to ETFs. Rates, limits and warning lists change several times a year, so the site works best as a plain-language layer that links to live regulator pages rather than as a store of numbers that will go stale.
